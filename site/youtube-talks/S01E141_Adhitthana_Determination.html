Adhiṭṭhāna — Determination

Bhante Bodhidhamma 20:03 YouTube Talks
Source: YouTube

Bhante Bodhidhamma examines adhiṭṭhāna — determination or resolution — as one of the ten pāramī (perfections) essential for awakening. He explores the Pali etymology of adhiṭṭhāna, breaking it down as 'higher establishment' and connecting it to the familiar term satipaṭṭhāna (establishment of awareness).

Drawing from the Visuddhimagga, Bhante explains adhiṭṭhāna as 'an unshakable resolve directed towards a wholesome goal, grounded in wisdom.' He demonstrates how determination serves as a binding force that brings together all ten pāramī — from dāna (generosity) and sīla (virtue) to paññā (wisdom) and upekkhā (equanimity) — in service of liberation.

The talk addresses the Buddha's own journey from extreme ascetic practices to the middle way, illustrating the difference between harmful willpower and skillful perseverance. Bhante emphasizes developing determination through small, consistent resolutions rather than overwhelming commitments, noting how keeping simple daily practices builds the 'muscle of perseverance.' He concludes with the arahant's declaration: 'What had to be done has been done' — the profound satisfaction of completing the spiritual task through unwavering resolve.
