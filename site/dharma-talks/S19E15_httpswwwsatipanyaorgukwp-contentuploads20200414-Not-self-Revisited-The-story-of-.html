Not-self Revisited: The Story of Sati (MN 38)

Bhante Bodhidhamma 20:41 Dharma Talks
Source: satipanya.org.uk

Bhante Bodhidhamma continues his exploration of not-self (anattā) through the powerful story of Bhikkhu Sati from the Mahātaṇhāsaṅkhaya Sutta (MN 38). This discourse addresses one of the most subtle misunderstandings about consciousness and identity in Buddhist teaching.

The talk examines different types of identity we hold - social, professional, and most importantly, existential identity: what we think we are in the face of death. Bhante explains how Sati mistakenly believed that consciousness (viññāṇa) transmigrates from life to life, confusing the dependently arisen nature of consciousness with an eternal self. The Buddha's firm correction emphasizes that consciousness arises only through the meeting of sense base, object, and contact - it has no independent existence.

Using the five khandhas framework, Bhante clarifies how we mistakenly identify with body, feelings, perceptions, mental formations (saṅkhāra), and consciousness itself. He explores the subtle delusion of confusing our intuitive awareness with the 'screen' of consciousness, comparing it to being absorbed in a film. This teaching offers crucial insights for understanding what the Buddha meant by 'self' and why recognizing not-self is essential for liberation from suffering.
