The Hindrances: The Psychotherapy of Vipassanā

Bhante Bodhidhamma 53:54 Dharma Talks
Source: satipanya.org.uk

In this comprehensive teaching from a 2011 Gaia House retreat, Bhante Bodhidhamma presents the five hindrances (nīvaraṇa) as the Buddha's categorization of all human suffering: sensual desire (kāmacchanda), aversion and fear (vyāpāda), dullness and lethargy (thīna-middha), restlessness with remorse (uddhacca-kukkucca), and skeptical doubt (vicikicchā). Drawing from the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta's instructions, he explains that liberation requires knowing how these mental states arise, how to undermine them, and how to prevent their future arising.

Bhante emphasizes the critical role of the thinking and imaginative mind in generating these hindrances, teaching that the essential practice involves coming off the thinking mind and plunging into the heart to feel what motivates our mental turbulence. He introduces the key technique of creating internal objectivity—pushing mental states outward to observe them, thereby breaking our mistaken identification with emotions. This process of disidentification allows us to discover that happiness, fear, anger, and other states are simply forms of energy (vedanā), not our true self.

The teaching covers practical strategies for each hindrance: recognizing the aftermath of indulgence (addiction, frustration, grief, fear of loss, and boredom); feeling fear without panicking by noting the 'fear of fear'; using body scanning to work with restlessness; refusing annihilation when dullness arises; and examining the fear underlying skeptical doubt. Bhante stresses that tackling our strongest hindrance undermines the entire attitude, since all hindrances share the same root of wrong identification.

Throughout, Bhante connects meditation practice to daily life, explaining that by finding our resting place in 'the one who knows'—our Buddha nature—we gradually lose our neediness for the world to provide happiness, discovering instead an unchangeable peace beyond these passing mental states.
