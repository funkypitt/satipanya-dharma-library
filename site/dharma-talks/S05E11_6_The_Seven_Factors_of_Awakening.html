The Seven Factors of Awakening

Bhante Bodhidhamma 38:46 Dharma Talks
Source: satipanya.org.uk

In this teaching from Gaia House, Bhante Bodhidhamma offers a comprehensive exploration of the seven factors of awakening (satta bojjhaṅgā) and their relationship to the five spiritual faculties (indriya). He explains how these factors—awareness (sati), investigation (dhammavicaya), effort (vīriya), interest (pīti), calmness (passaddhi), concentration (samādhi), and equanimity (upekkhā)—must be carefully balanced for insight to arise.

Bhante emphasizes the crucial distinction between Buddhist confidence (saddhā) and mere belief, explaining that the Buddha's path requires personal realization rather than blind faith. He outlines the three stages of wisdom (paññā): received knowledge from hearing the teachings, intellectual understanding through reflection, and direct realization through vipassanā practice.

A central theme is the balance between active and passive qualities in meditation. Too much effort leads to restlessness and potential collapse; too little results in unconsciousness while appearing outwardly composed. Drawing from his own teaching experience, Bhante reflects on how emphasizing calmness and genuine interest—rather than forceful striving—proved more effective for Western practitioners. He uses the Buddha's famous simile of tuning a stringed instrument: neither too tight nor too slack.

The teaching concludes with the importance of equanimity in investigation, warning against preconceived ideas about spiritual experiences. Bhante stresses that when awareness is properly established at the 'observation post,' all seven factors naturally arise to support it—making moment-to-moment awareness sufficient for the entire practice.
