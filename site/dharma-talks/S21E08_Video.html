The Five Remembrances: Contemplating Aging, Sickness, Death, Loss, and Kamma

Bhante Bodhidhamma 39:39 Dharma Talks
Source: satipanya.org.uk

In this practical teaching, Bhante Bodhidhamma leads a guided contemplation on the Five Remembrances (pañcānussati) — the Buddha's recommended daily reflections on aging, sickness, death, separation from loved ones, and kamma. Drawing from the Upajjhāṭṭhāna Sutta (AN 5.57), Bhante explains how these contemplations serve not as morbid exercises but as powerful tools for awakening our motivation to practice and for cultivating genuine equanimity.

Bhante offers two approaches to working with these reflections: simply repeating the phrases while noticing our heart's response, or using imagination to bring specific fears to mind — whether Alzheimer's, cancer, or losing those we love. The key practice is receiving whatever fear, grief, or aversion arises with care and compassion, as one would comfort a suffering child. This transforms intellectual understanding into heart acceptance.

The teaching emphasises balance, pairing these sobering reflections with gratitude contemplations — for our parents, teachers, medical practitioners, the gifts of culture and nature, and the preciousness of human life and the Dhamma. Bhante notes that according to the Buddha, death contemplation alone can be a path to nibbāna. Paradoxically, fully accepting mortality makes life more precious and wonderful. When we stop denying death, we find equanimity and inner serenity — not through control, but through acceptance of things as they are.
