Investigating Not-self: The Practice

Bhante Bodhidhamma 23:39 Dharma Talks
Source: satipanya.org.uk

In this practical teaching, Bhante Bodhidhamma moves beyond the intellectual understanding of anattā (not-self) to present three specific meditation techniques for direct investigation. Drawing on the Buddha's second discourse on not-self and the Satipaṭṭhāna framework, he explains how to deconstruct experiences like physical pain and emotions into their basic sensational components, revealing their constructed nature.

The first technique involves deconstructing phenomena by examining what pain or emotions are actually made of - heat, pressure, tightness - rather than accepting them as solid realities. The second explores our lack of control over arising experiences, undermining the sense of ownership. The third technique objectifies all experiences, pulling back into the observer position where everything manifests as 'not me, not mine, not a self.'

Using the developmental psychology of infant perception and the myth of Narcissus as teaching metaphors, Bhante illustrates how we construct our sense of self and world, and how vipassanā practice reverses this process. He emphasizes that this intuitive awareness naturally knows how to investigate when we step back from trying to control the process. This talk offers practical guidance for intermediate practitioners ready to apply not-self contemplation directly in their sitting meditation.
