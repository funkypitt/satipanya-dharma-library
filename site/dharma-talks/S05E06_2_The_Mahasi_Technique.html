The Mahasi Technique

Bhante Bodhidhamma 48:59 Dharma Talks
Source: satipanya.org.uk

In this detailed practical talk, Bhante Bodhidhamma unpacks the Mahasi Sayadaw technique of vipassanā meditation, explaining its three essential components: noting, noting intentions, and slowing down. He contextualises this within the Buddha's own journey to awakening, emphasising that the purpose of right awareness (sammā sati) is not to eliminate life's pleasures but to find a different relationship with them—one that doesn't depend on impermanent phenomena for lasting contentment.

Bhante explains how the noting technique keeps the intellect contained through simple, concrete words while pushing attention toward direct experience. He uses the analogy of a child shouting 'bird!' to illustrate how words help attention stick to objects. The note should be deliberate and concurrent with experience, not mechanical like a mantra. When noting feels too loud and obscures direct experience, practitioners learn to 'sneak around' the word to contact the underlying sensation.

The talk offers rich practical guidance for daily life practice: stopping at doorways to release accumulated emotions, noting intentions before actions, and deliberately slowing down to see mental and physical processes clearly. Bhante emphasises that intention—an idea empowered with desire—is where all action begins, and catching intentions before they manifest creates moments of discernment and freedom.

Particularly valuable is his teaching on the difference between suppressing emotions and 'parking' them—knowing something is present without adding further turbulence. He encourages practitioners to maintain continuous investigation throughout the day, reminding us that retreat conditions demonstrate how quickly habits can change when we apply consistent effort.
