Investigative Eating: The Practice

Bhante Bodhidhamma 12:29 Dharma Talks
Source: satipanya.org.uk

In this practical teaching, Bhante Bodhidhamma offers detailed guidance on using the daily act of eating as a powerful opportunity for insight meditation. He explains how to establish the right attitude before eating—viewing food as medicine for the body rather than a source of lasting happiness—and then systematically deconstruct the experience into its component parts: bodily sensations, emotional responses, and the arising of intention.

The talk emphasizes staying close to direct experience at the sense bases, using simple noting like "tasting" to remain present with what is actually happening rather than filtering experience through past concepts. Bhante guides practitioners to observe the moment when the body signals sufficiency, and to notice the familiar pull of desire that wants to override this signal. By attending to this wanting without feeding it, practitioners can discover the crucial distinction between gratification (temporarily satisfying desire) and contentment (the mind free from desire)—one leading toward liberation, the other perpetuating the cycle of craving.

Bhante also addresses the practical dilemma of leftover food, suggesting that experiencing guilt mindfully can become a guardian for future restraint. He concludes with instruction on post-meal practice: cultivating calm abiding through relaxation, steady attention, and receptive awareness. This receptive quality, he notes, naturally gives rise to insight, as awareness and intuitive intelligence are two sides of the same coin—one passive and receiving, the other active in understanding. The teaching connects directly to yoniso manasikāra (wise attention) as essential to the path of awakening.
