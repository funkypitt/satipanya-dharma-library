The Five Remembrances: Contemplating Aging, Sickness, and Death

Bhante Bodhidhamma 39:39 Dharma Talks
Source: satipanya.org.uk

In this practical guided session, Bhante Bodhidhamma leads practitioners through the Five Remembrances (pañcānussati), a powerful contemplative practice the Buddha taught to awaken our motivation for Dhamma practice. These reflections address aging, sickness, death, separation from loved ones, and kamma—topics our death-phobic culture typically avoids.

Bhante offers two approaches to this practice: simply repeating each phrase and allowing its truth to penetrate the heart, or using imagination to bring specific fears to mind. He emphasises receiving whatever emotional responses arise—fear, grief, aversion—with care and compassion, as one would comfort a suffering child. This transforms intellectual understanding into genuine heart-acceptance.

The teaching draws on the Visuddhimagga's methodology while adding practical guidance on working with resistance. Bhante explains how contemplating kamma has both dark and bright aspects, recognising that we inherit the results of both wholesome and unwholesome actions. Crucially, he balances these sobering reflections with gratitude practice—for our birth, family, teachers, medical practitioners, society, nature, and the precious Dhamma itself.

Bhante concludes with the profound paradox: accepting impermanence makes life more precious, not less. According to the Buddha, death contemplation alone can be a path to nibbāna. When we stop denying death, we find equanimity, inner serenity, and freedom from being surprised when difficulties arise.
