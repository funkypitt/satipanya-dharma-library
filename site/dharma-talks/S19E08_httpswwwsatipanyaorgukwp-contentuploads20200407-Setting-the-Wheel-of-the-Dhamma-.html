Setting the Wheel of the Dhamma in Motion

Bhante Bodhidhamma 24:53 Dharma Talks
Source: satipanya.org.uk

In this profound exploration of the Buddha's first formal discourse, Bhante Bodhidhamma examines the Dhammacakkappavattana Sutta (SN 56.11) - the famous "Setting in Motion the Wheel of the Dhamma." This foundational teaching presents the Buddha's discovery of the Middle Way, transcending both sensual indulgence and self-mortification, and introduces the Four Noble Truths in their classical formulation.

Bhante carefully unpacks each Noble Truth: dukkha (suffering/unsatisfactoriness) encompassing birth, aging, illness, death, and the five aggregates subject to clinging; taṇhā (craving) as the root cause including desires for sensual pleasure, becoming, and annihilation; the cessation of suffering through abandoning unwholesome reactivity; and the Noble Eightfold Path as the way forward. He emphasizes how each truth requires specific action - understanding suffering, abandoning its cause, realizing cessation, and developing the path.

The talk includes the beautiful concluding imagery of the cosmic celebration when Venerable Koṇḍañña became the first to gain the "dust-free, stainless vision of the Dhamma," earning the name Añña Koṇḍañña ("Koṇḍañña who knows"). This teaching provides both newcomers and experienced practitioners with essential foundations for understanding the Buddha's core message of liberation from suffering.

When the wheel of the Dhamma had been set in motion by the Blessed One, the earth-dwelling Devas raised a cry: "At Varanasi, in the deer park of Isipatana, the unsurpassed wheel of the Dhamma has been set in motion by the Blessed One, which cannot be stopped by any ascetic or Brahmin or Deva or Mara or Brahma or anyone in the world."

Having heard the cry of the earth-dwelling Devas, the Devas of the realms of the four kings raised the same cry. And from there to the Tavatimsa Devas and the Yama Devas, the Tusita Devas, the Nimmanarati Devas, and the Devas of the Brahma's company raised the cry: "At Varanasi, in the deer park at Isipatana, this unsurpassed wheel of Dhamma has been set in motion by the Blessed One, which cannot be stopped by any ascetic or Brahmin or Deva or Mara or Brahma or anyone in that world."

Thus, at that moment, at that instant, at that second, the cry spread as far as the Brahma world, and this ten-thousand-fold world system shook, quaked and trembled, and an immeasurable glorious radiance appeared in the world, surpassing the divine majesty of the Devas.

Then the Blessed One uttered this inspired utterance: "Kondanya has indeed understood. Kondanya has indeed understood." And in this way, the Venerable Kondanya acquired the name Anya Kondanya - Kondanya, the one who knows.

So that's a lovely poetic remembrance of the effect of turning the wheel of the Dhamma for us poor human beings.
