### CASE 1: Fill Seven Factors of Awakening (0/2 → 2/2) — STRONGLY RECOMMENDED

This is the only completely empty theme in the book. Two excellent existing
dharma-talks were never selected (they predated the `select` pass or were
overlooked). Both are among Bhante's finest work.

#### Slot A: S05E10 — "The Seven Factors of Awakening"

- **Quality: 9/10** | 5,294 words | dharma-talks
- **Teaching**: Comprehensive exploration of all seven bojjhaṅga (awareness,
  effort, concentration, calmness, joy/interest, equanimity, investigation).
  Also covers the five spiritual faculties and three types of dukkha.
- **Narrative strength**: Exceptional. Bhante admits past mistakes ("heavily
  intensive practice with poor results"), tells the story of a student who
  spent £10,000 on crack cocaine after abandoning practice due to excessive
  effort, uses the vīṇā string tuning analogy, and recounts a monk taught
  by a child through an anthill riddle.
- **Why include**: This is the theoretical foundation — *what* the seven
  factors are, *why* they matter, and *how* they relate to the hindrances.
  The personal teaching history (harsh Gaia House retreats → compassionate
  approach) gives it unusual authenticity.

#### Slot B: S06E01 — "Developing the Factors of Enlightenment"

- **Quality: 9/10** | 5,335 words | dharma-talks
- **Teaching**: Practical meditation manual for cultivating the seven factors.
  Walking meditation with noting (rising, falling, touching), balancing
  active vs. passive factors, troubleshooting signs of over-effort
  (restlessness) and under-effort (drowsiness).
- **Narrative strength**: Excellent. Depressives Anonymous meeting where
  people reinforce their depression; a man who mistook a toilet experience
  for Nibbāna; Luther's insight while evacuating; a scholar-monk unable to
  recognize liberation until taught by a child.
- **Why include**: Complements S05E10 perfectly — this is the *practice*
  manual. Together they provide theory + application, a natural pair for
  the two-slot quota.

**Verdict**: Both should be added. They fill the book's largest gap with
outstanding material that was inexplicably overlooked.

### CASE 2: Fill Daily Life & Integration (3/5 → 5/5) — RECOMMENDED

Two vacancies, two new YouTube candidates.

#### Slot A: S01E147 — "Good Friendship" (MN commentary)

- **Quality: 8.5/10** | 2,127 words | youtube-talks
- **Teaching**: Seven qualities of a friend (sacrifice, endurance,
  confidentiality, etc.), four types of helpful friends, the Buddha's
  statement that good friendship is "the entire spiritual life, not just half."
- **Narrative strength**: Uses structured questioning ("Would they?… Would
  we?") that forces self-reflection. Tells of a marriage where one partner
  became Buddhist and the other felt threatened (eventual resolution).
  COVID lockdown context: how neighbours help during crisis.
- **Why include**: Directly addresses how lay practitioners navigate
  relationships while on the path. Highly practical. Complements existing
  selections (Meditation and Daily Life, Investigative Eating, New Year
  Reflections) by adding the relationship dimension.

#### Slot B: S01E184 — "At Sāmagāma Discourse" (MN 104)

- **Quality: 8/10** | 2,660 words | youtube-talks
- **Teaching**: Six roots of disputes (anger, contempt, envy, deceit, wrong
  view, inability to negotiate), seven settlement methods (confrontation,
  majority opinion, burden of proof, mental-state consideration, confession,
  character pronouncement, "covering with grass"). Drawn from the Buddha
  preparing his community for his passing.
- **Narrative strength**: Self-aware humour about "Buddhist propaganda"
  regarding the Jain schism. Parallels to modern contexts: workplace
  conflicts, Brexit negotiations, Oliver Sacks' neurological case studies.
- **Why include**: Maps ancient dispute resolution onto modern
  work/community/family life. More intellectually oriented than "Good
  Friendship" — together they cover both the relational and systemic
